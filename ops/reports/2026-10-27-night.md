## Later (04:48 UTC): why the MTDI page was slow in the tour

### Bugs found and fixed
* **The tour browsed every page with the startup intro still running.** A visitor can't reach the nav during the intro, because the header ignores clicks until the intro is scrolled away. The tour's `element.click()` got around that. The intro's full-screen shader and the hidden field background then kept drawing behind every page.
  * Measured: opened directly, the MTDI page was ready in 8.7 s. Going Home, then Page 1, then MTDI, it took 263 s.
  * The tour now wheels through the intro on Home first, and reports a problem if the intro never finishes.

### Weaknesses
* **The studio's always-on shell is heavy without a GPU.** Under every page, two full-screen background shaders and the full-screen scrub canvas draw every frame. In software GL (this container has no GPU; Chromium uses SwiftShader), that holds every animation loop near 1.5 frames a second, so a 3D page takes minutes to settle.
  * With the intro gone, the MTDI page took 42-161 s on the desktop pass and 189-273 s on the phone pass. Runs vary a lot.
  * The tour now waits up to 420 s per page, a measurement budget rather than a speed target.
  * Nothing leaks between pages: Page 1's loops are gone on the MTDI page.

### Hardware the owner needs to do
* **On the Pi, open the MTDI page and say whether its animations look smooth or stutter.** The Pi has a GPU, so it should do far better than this container, but nobody has checked. If it stutters, the always-on background shaders are the first thing to cut back.

### Features
* Every D4 card on every page now has real words instead of placeholders: titles, notes, buttons, input hints, dropdowns, readout rows and empty states. That covers the engine section, each Page 1 tab, the sweep, environment, validation and Settings. Channel names and units come from the project's words.
* Buttons say what they do: FIT versus FIT AND APPLY, CAPTURE A BLOCK, TAKE REFERENCE, ADD TRIAL. Destructive ones (CLEAR, DELETE) have a tooltip saying what they remove.

### Bugs found and fixed
* **D4 read a fed channel 3 sweep as a radio sweep.** A project's sweep reached the D4 sweep analysis, which then labelled it in MHz and dBm. It also gave a density per 100 MHz, a "consistent with the … band" note, and a list of peaks outside known radio bands. The analysis now keeps the sweep's own x unit, and the card reads the sweep in the project's units without the radio-only rows. The fingerprint card does the same.
* **The exposure card's source note said the ICNIRP 1998 public level "was 100 unit".** It was 100 µT. A rename pass had replaced the unit.
* **Calibration readouts wrote a literal "unit" after every offset, whatever the project.** They now use the project's channel 1 unit; the default words still read "unit" until a project names one.

* **CodeRabbit on this PR: 3 findings, then 5, 3 and 2 more on the fixes, all fixed.**
  * The D4 sweep analysis did decibel arithmetic (the floor threshold, the occupied width) on a fed sweep whatever its level unit. It now analyses a fed sweep only when its levels are in dB, and otherwise says so.
  * It rounded fed positions to whole numbers, which is right for hertz and wrong for a fed x-axis (1.25 mm became 1). Fed positions now keep their decimals.
  * The fingerprint compared a radio reference's floor with a fed sweep's floor. A floor change is now stated only between two sweeps of the same kind. A missing floor used to count as 0 in the subtraction; it now gives no change.
  * Second review: the fingerprint also records the fed level unit, so dBm and dBFS floors are never subtracted. A floor change always reads dB. A fed peak is no longer turned into a GHz feature for the reference match and the classifier. A peak at position 0 shows.
  * Third review: channel 1's values are labelled in their source's unit (a feed's own, else the magnetometer's µT), not the project's configured label, in the calibration, noise, fingerprint and detection limits. Fed positions show all six decimals the analysis keeps. The repeatability run names the project's channels. Fourth review: a feed that names no unit reads in the project's channel 1 word, not µT. A calibration fit keeps the unit it was made in.

### Weaknesses
* The D4 engine itself is still built around the studio's own instrument: a magnetometer in µT on channel 1 and an E-field probe on channel 2. With a project's words, the cards say the project's names, but the physics underneath (line frequency, exposure in µT and V/m) is that instrument's. That is honest for the studio's probe and meaningless for, say, a temperature project. Making D4 project-aware is a design item, not a words fix.
